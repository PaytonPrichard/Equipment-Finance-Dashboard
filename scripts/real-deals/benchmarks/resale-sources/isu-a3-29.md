## 3. What the % means: verbatim quotes

Basis (new list price), p. 1, right column:
> "Estimates of the remaining value of tractors and other classes of farm machines as a percent of new list price are listed in Tables 1a and 1b. Note that for tractors, combines and forage harvesters the number of hours of annual use is also considered when estimating the remaining value."

Data source and value type (auction, as-is), p. 1, right column (continues directly from the quote above):
> "The factors were developed from published reports of used equipment auction values, and are estimates of the average "as-is" value of a class of machines in average mechanical condition at the farm. Actual market value will vary from these values depending on the condition of the machine, the current market for new machines, and local preferences or dislikes for certain models."

Multiply by list price of a replacement, even if bought below list, p. 1, right column:
> "The appropriate values in Table 1 should be multiplied by the current manufacturer's list price of a replacement machine of equivalent size and type, even if the actual machine was or will be purchased for less than its list price."

Table attribution, p. 2 (below Table 1b):
> "Source: American Society of Agricultural and Biological Engineers (ASABE)."

Salvage value definition (trade-in / used market / zero), p. 1, right column:
> "Salvage value is an estimate of the sale value of the machine at the end of its economic life. It is the amount you could expect to receive as a trade-in allowance, an estimate of the used market value if you expect to sell the machine outright, or zero if you plan to keep the machine until it is worn out."

Economic life rule of thumb, p. 1, right column:
> "A good rule of thumb is to use an economic life of 10 to 12 years for most farm machines and a 15-year life for tractors, unless you know you will trade sooner."

Using the factors for a used machine's current value, p. 7, left column:
> "When estimating future costs for a machine that you have already owned for several years, start with your best estimate of the current market value of the machine instead of its original purchase price, or use the salvage value factors in Table 1 to estimate its current value."

Observation (not a quote): the publication says the tables are ASABE-sourced (p. 2) and also that the factors "were developed from published reports of used equipment auction values" (p. 1). It does not say who developed them from auction data, which years of auction data, or the ASABE standard number.

## 4. Purchase price vs list price (dealer discount)

The only numeric discount in the publication is in the worked example, p. 3, left column:
> "The example is a 300-PTO horsepower diesel tractor with a list price of $400,000. Dealer discounts are assumed to reduce the actual purchase price to $350,000."

That is an assumed 12.5% discount for the example ($50,000 / $400,000, my arithmetic). The publication states it as an assumption for the example only, not as a typical market discount. No general statement on typical dealer discounts appears anywhere in the document. Typical discount: null.

Related quote, p. 1 (repeated from section 3): "...even if the actual machine was or will be purchased for less than its list price."

Example application, p. 3, left column:
> "For the 300-hp tractor with 400 hours of annual use in the example, the salvage value after 12 years is estimated as 28% of the new list price"
> "Salvage value = current list price × remaining value factor (Table 1a) = $400,000 × 28% = $112,000"
> "Total depreciation = purchase price - salvage value = $350,000 - $112,000 = $238,000"

(The × sign renders as a replacement character in the extracted text. Read as multiplication from context and the arithmetic.)

## Cross-checks performed

- Table 1a, 150+ hp, 400 hrs, age 12 = 28%. Matches the p. 3 example ("28% of the new list price").
- Table 1b, Plows, age 14 = 30%. Matches the p. 6 chisel plow example ("From Table 1b, the expected salvage value at the end of 14 years is 30% of the current list price of an equivalent machine (estimated to be $100,000), or $30,000."). The source uses the Plows column for a chisel plow.
- Spot cells compared between `-raw` and `-table` output: 30-79 hp/200 age 1 = 65%, 80-149 hp/600 age 20 = 23%, Combine/500 age 20 = 5%, Combine/100 age 1 = 79%, Vehicle age 13 = 24%, Other age 1 = 69%, Swather/Rake age 20 = 15%. All identical in both modes.

## Problems found in the source

1. Worksheet example (p. 10), line J for the chisel plow shows "31% $ 30,000". The text (p. 6) and arithmetic ($30,000 / $100,000) give 30%. Table 1b Plows age 14 is 30%. The 31% on the worksheet appears to be an error in the source. Use the table value.
2. Worksheet example (p. 10), line R for the chisel plow shows end-of-life repair "31%". The text (p. 7) says end-of-life is 45% and the difference is 31%. Source error in the worksheet, not relevant to Tables 1a/1b.
3. pdftotext `-layout` output shifts Table 1a and 1b rows by one versus the age labels. Anyone re-extracting should use `-raw` or `-table`.
