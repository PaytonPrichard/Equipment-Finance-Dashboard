# Public equipment ABS benchmarks, October 2026

Pool-level statistics from the three equipment ABS programs that file full public prospectuses on EDGAR, compared with Tranche's built-in equipment assumptions. Other programs (Kubota, Volvo, Dell, HPE, Navistar, Caterpillar) appear only in fund holdings or ABS-15G filings, which marks them as 144A deals with no public prospectus.

**What this can and cannot show.** These are captive-finance pools of thousands of small retail contracts to farmers, contractors and truckers. They test Tranche's sector and structure assumptions. They say nothing about any single borrower. Loss definitions and measurement dates differ across programs (noted below), so compare orders of magnitude, not decimals.

Researched 2026-10-09 by three agents from the final prospectuses (and, for Daimler, the latest 10-D reports). Figures as printed unless marked derived.

## 1. Losses by asset type (Joel's ranking vs Tranche)

Expectations, recorded before the data (`expectations.json`): Joel ranks trucks worst, then construction, then agriculture. Tranche scores Agriculture and Construction both "high" risk and Transportation/Logistics "moderate".

| | JDOT (75% ag, 25% construction) | CNH (87% ag, 13% construction) | DTRT (trucks: 90% tractors) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative net loss, 2023 vintage at about 3 years | 2023-B: 0.68% at month 35 | 2023-A: 0.76% at month 36 | 2023-1: 4.11% at period 36 (10-D) |
| Cumulative net loss, 2024 vintage | 2024: 0.63% at month 27 | 2024-A: 0.69% at month 27 | 2024-1: 2.91% at period 29 (10-D) |
| Cumulative net loss, 2021-2022 vintages | 0.16% to 0.36% at months 43-45 | 0.20% to 0.69% at months 41-52 | 2022-1: 3.23% at period 36 (10-D) |
| Pre-2021 vintages | n/a | n/a | 2018-1 1.17%, 2019-1 1.04%, 2020-1 0.35% (~33-35 months) |
| Sponsor portfolio net losses, % of average portfolio | FY2021 0.22, FY2022 0.09, FY2023 0.09, FY2024 0.26, FY2025 0.50 | 2022 0.11, 2023 0.17, 2024 0.32, 2025 0.56 | 2019 0.71, 2020 0.97, 2021 0.30, 2022 0.01, 2023 0.91 |
| Senior hard credit enhancement (derived) | ~3.5% of note value (2.5% OC + 1.0% reserve) | ~4.5% of pool (2.5% OC + 2.0% spread account) | ~9.0% of adjusted pool (8.75% OC + 0.25% reserve), ~12.9% incl. yield supplement |

**Findings**

- **Trucks lose far more than ag and construction.** At the same age and vintage year, truck pools lost 4 to 6 times as much (2023 vintage: 4.1% vs 0.7-0.8%). Trucks also need about 2 to 3 times the credit enhancement for AAA. This agrees with Joel's ranking and **contradicts Tranche's tiers**, which score trucking (Transportation/Logistics) better than agriculture.
- **Agriculture vs construction cannot be separated.** Neither JDOT nor CNH splits losses by equipment type, and construction is a stable 10-25% of every pool. CNH (more ag) lost more than JDOT (less ag), but lender differences confound that. **Inconclusive.**
- **Ag-heavy pools are low-loss in absolute terms:** 0.1-0.6% a year, even in the recent rise. That is hard to square with "high" risk.
- **All three are deteriorating.** Ag and construction portfolio losses roughly quintupled from 2022-23 to 2025. Truck vintages from 2022-24 run 3-4% against about 1% before 2020 (the freight downturn). Benchmarks taken in one year of the cycle will drift.

## 3. New vs used

JDOT 53.5% new / 46.5% used. CNH 52% / 48% (used share up from about 30% in 2023). DTRT 87% / 13%. None of the three discloses losses by new vs used, so **Tranche's 15% used-equipment haircut cannot be tested** with public ABS data.

## 4. Down payment and advance rate

| | Policy | Pool data |
|---|---|---|
| JDOT | Minimum down: ag 20% (seasonal) or 10% (monthly), construction 10% new / 15% used. Ag normally financed up to 90% of dealer invoice (max 110%). Construction 100-115% of dealer invoice. | None at contract level |
| CNH | Amount financed up to 125% of value (new: dealer invoice cost; used: third-party valuation), underwriter overrides allowed | Weighted average advance rate 88.36%. 31.7% of balance above 100%, 33.6% at or below 81% |
| DTRT | No stated minimum. Down payment used as a structuring lever. | None |

**Finding.** Tranche's defaults (10-15% down, LTV factor targeting 85%, 100% LTV hard gate on cost) line up with captive practice: 10-20% down guidelines and an 88% average advance. Two caveats. Captives advance against dealer *invoice*, which sits below retail. And they routinely lend above 100% of invoice on construction, backed by manufacturer support and dealer recourse that an independent lender does not get. So Tranche's 100% gate is conservative relative to captives, which is appropriate for a non-captive lender.

## Decisions for Joel

1. **Industry tiers.** The evidence says trucking should not score better than agriculture. Options: move Transportation/Logistics to high; split out a Trucking sector at high and leave logistics and rail at moderate; and/or move Agriculture to moderate. Ag vs construction stays unresolved.
2. **Down payment and LTV defaults.** Supported as they stand. No change recommended.
3. **Used haircut and term coverage.** Untestable with public ABS data. They need another source, such as auction or residual value data.
